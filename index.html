5Module lines

The pattern repeats: the next pillar has the same bracket line, and the last I keeps the minimum spacing to it.

No bracket line. I distances start from the left pillar.

Every chapel has 5 module lines: the bracket line and 4 I, or 5 I without a bracket line. They alternate between module positions A and B over the whole greenhouse.

The other I are spread evenly up to the bracket line on the next pillar (or the far pillar if there is no bracket line).

Each I gets a position you can find on the structure without measuring: a U-bolt against the inside of a triangle leg, a U-bolt centred between two triangles, or a plate end touching a gutter or culture line. All module lines keep the minimum spacing and stay as close as possible to an even spread. I that cannot be referenced this way get a free position.

      How the simulation works

      Every ray, every LED

      The face of each nearby LED is split into up to 15 × 2 points, so thin Voltiris parts cast the soft shadow a real fixture casts. From each point, rays leave in every direction below the fixture, weighted by the beam shape. The light on a spot of the crop adds up the rays of all LEDs within six chapels and eight truss bays.

      E = Σ Φ · I(γ, C) · T(γ, C) · cos γ / d²

      Voltiris parts cast shadows

      The module, receiver, tracker, I-structure, profile, rods and optimisers are boxes taken from our 3D model, placed on the module lines of the Structure tab. A ray that hits one is stopped (T = 0). The lamellas let PAR through. With Voltiris off, the same LEDs give the reference.

      Beam shapes

      Most makers publish only a beam angle. We then use the smooth curve with half its peak at that angle (cosⁿ γ). Where a measured curve exists we use it, and you can load an IES or LDT file. Real optics can be wider than the smooth curve: ask the maker for the file.
